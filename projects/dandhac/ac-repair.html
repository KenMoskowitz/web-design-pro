What we fix

Common AC problems, solved today.

If you're seeing one of these, you're not alone — and you're not waiting until tomorrow.

Warm air from the vents

Usually low refrigerant, a frozen evaporator coil, or a failing compressor. We diagnose which one — and don't sell you a system replacement to solve a $200 problem.

AC won't turn on at all

Tripped breaker, dead capacitor, bad thermostat, or a blown low-voltage fuse. We carry the parts on the truck. Most no-power calls are resolved in one visit.

Ice on the unit or copper lines

Restricted airflow or low refrigerant — both will burn out your compressor if ignored. Turn the system off, call us, and we'll be there before the ice melts.

Loud or unusual noises

Banging, hissing, screeching, or buzzing each mean something different. None of them mean "ignore it." Most are cheap to fix early and expensive to fix late.

Water around the indoor unit

Clogged condensate line, broken drain pan, or a frozen coil that's now thawing. We clear, repair, and add a safety float switch so it doesn't ruin your ceiling next time.

High electric bills

If your AC is running fine but the bill is climbing, the system is working harder than it should. Often a tune-up, refrigerant top-up, or duct seal pays for itself in two months.

Repair vs. replace

When a repair is right. When it isn't.

Other companies will tell you to replace because new systems pay them better. We'll tell you the truth — and most of the time, the truth is "repair it, you've got years left." Here's the rule of thumb we use in your home:

  • → Unit under 10 years old? Almost always repair.
  • → Repair cost under 30% of replacement? Repair.
  • → Refrigerant is R-22 and the leak is major? Replace makes sense.
  • → Compressor failure on a 12+ year old unit? Replace — but we'll show you the math.

Free second opinions.

Got a quote from another contractor for a full system replacement? Bring it to us. We'll do a free in-home review and a real Manual J load calculation. About 30% of the time, the replacement isn't necessary — and we'll tell you so even though it costs us the sale.
